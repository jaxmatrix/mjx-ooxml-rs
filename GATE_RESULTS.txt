THE RED PASSES, RECORDED
=================================================================================================

A record that mentions only the green run is not a record. Every one of these was a real failure
during this child, and each is written with what it was and what it cost.

 1. `cargo build -p mjx-layout-chart` — 17 errors, first compile.
    `mjx-dml`'s generated attribute accessors return `Result<_, AttributeError>` and not `Option`,
    which is a real difference between `mjx-chart`'s hand-written readers and `mjx-derive`'s
    generated ones. Nine of the seventeen were that.

 2. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test ticks_are_arithmetic` — 1 of 10 red.
    `a_hundred_percent_stacked_axis_is_always_a_proportion` expected
    `0.6000000000000001` on the assumption that the tick arithmetic would drift. It does not: the
    step's integer mantissa gives exactly `0.6`. The **expectation** was wrong and the code was
    right, which is the good direction for that to happen in.

 3. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test a_hierarchy_is_laid_out` — 1 of 10 red.
    **A real defect.** `a_cyclic_parent_graph_terminates` reported `DiagramHasNoPoints` for two
    points that each claim to be the other's parent: the breadth-first walk seeded its queue from
    the points with no parent, and a cycle has none, so *every* node was dropped. Fixed by
    restarting the walk from the first unreached point, as many times as it takes. A file
    round-tripped through a repair tool can carry such a cycle, and before this the whole diagram
    would have vanished.

 4. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test the_furniture_is_drawn` — 1 of 9 red.
    `error_bars_reach_above_and_below_each_point` asserted strict straddling for every point. The
    top point sits **on** the axis' maximum, so its upper bar is clamped there. The engine is right
    and the assertion was too strong; it now asserts the clamp explicitly and keeps a strict
    straddle for a point away from the maximum.

 5. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test fragments_reach_the_tree` — 1 of 5 red.
    `the_metric_a_host_hands_in_changes_the_plot_area` used a metric that was wider than
    `NominalMetrics` but *shorter*, so the title reserved less height rather than more. The probe
    was not discriminating in the dimension it claimed to test.

 6. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test the_seam_holds` — 1 of 5 red.
    `SOURCE_FILE_COUNT` said 11 and `src/` holds 12. That is the gate doing its job on its first
    run.

 7. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-chart --test no_panic_on_a_layout_path` — 1 of 9 red.
    `every_index_is_into_a_vector_this_crate_sized` found `palette.rs` indexing a slice and the
    allowlist named only two files. `accents[index % 6]` cannot be out of range; it is now on the
    list with that reason written out, which is the point of an allowlist over a suppression.

 8. `cargo test -p mjx-layout-pptx` — 1 of 116 red.
    `charts-slide1.txt` moved from 2 fragments to 51. **That is the ticket**: a real `.pptx` from
    the committed corpus now lays its chart out. The baseline was regenerated and read before
    committing — twelve gridlines, three bars, an axis with its ticks and labels.

 9. `cargo test -p xtask --test one_engine_three_formats` — the flagship, red twice.
    First: `different fragment counts: 42 against 20`, because the worksheet's chart root was found
    by "the first branch node", which is the sheet's own grid `TableFragment`. Now found by its
    **address** — a two-segment path, which is what a sheet gives an anchored object and nothing
    else has. Second: `ResizingBehavior::MoveAndResize` is spelled
    `MoveAndResizeWithAnchorCells` in this workspace, because every identifier here is
    self-explanatory.

10. **Mutation, deliberately introduced and confirmed red.** With Excel's chart frame shortened by
    one point, `one_chart_reaches_three_hosts_and_comes_out_the_same` fails at `fragment 1`. The
    mutation was reverted and the suite is green again. Without that check the comparison could
    have been vacuous — three empty subtrees compare equal.

11. `cargo fmt --all -- --check` — red on first run, in `mjx-layout-chart/src/diagram.rs` and four
    other files. Applied and re-checked.

12. `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings` — **14 errors in the new crate.** Six
    `assign_op_pattern`, five `clone_on_copy` (`Scale` is `Copy` and the clones were redundant), two
    `unnecessary closure with bool::then`, one `manual_is_multiple_of`; then four `useless_format`
    in the test fixtures on the next three passes.

13. `cargo test --workspace` — **1 failure out of 410 suites, and it is the finding of this child.**

        layering violation: `mjx-layout-pptx` (rank 3.6 (PowerPoint's box model)) declares
        `mjx-layout-chart` (rank 3.55 (the chart engine)) as a dependency. This one points upward.

    `Rank` held `major` and a bare `minor` and derived `Ord` on the pair, which is correct only for
    as long as every fraction has one digit — and MJXOFF-178 is the first rank with two. `Rank(3, 55)`
    compares **greater** than `Rank(3, 6)` because 55 is greater than 6.

    The gate was right to be red and the arithmetic was wrong. The fraction is now stated in
    hundredths at every call site (`Rank(3, 50)`, `Rank(3, 55)`, `Rank(3, 60)`), `Display` trims the
    trailing zero so every message still reads `3.6`, and
    `a_two_digit_fraction_sorts_and_prints_the_way_a_reader_reads_it` asserts both the ordering and
    the spelling. **Nothing had ever checked the rank arithmetic itself**; a two-digit fraction was
    always going to arrive, and the next one would have arrived the same way.

14. `RUSTDOCFLAGS=... cargo doc --workspace --no-deps` — 2 errors, both in `model.rs`'s own module
    header: an intra-doc link to a private `read_numbers` and one to a `read_labels` that had been
    inlined away three hours earlier. The prose was describing a function that no longer existed.

=================================================================================================
THE WEAKEST PART, STATED
=================================================================================================

**Chart text is measured rather than shaped.** All three hosts pass `NominalMetrics`, a per-character
advance table read off Calibri and grouped by this engine. Everything the negotiation reserves — the
tick-label gutter, the legend's width, the title's height — rests on it, so every rectangle in a laid-
out chart is a few percent off what a real font engine would give. It is deterministic, which is what
makes `one_engine_three_formats` an equality rather than an approximate comparison, and the seam for
a real metric (`TextMetrics`) is live and proved live by a test. But it is the one number in this
crate that is wrong on purpose, and it is upstream of every other number.

Second weakest: **the chart's own handles reach no resolver yet.** `mjx-scene-pptx` and
`mjx-scene-xlsx` answer `None` for a handle at or above `CHART_HANDLE_BASE`, which is correct — the
chart table is a different table — and means a chart's fills and outlines are addressable and not yet
resolvable. Teaching the two companions (and writing `mjx-scene-docx`) is a follow-up, not a defect
here, but a chart does not reach pixels until it happens.
